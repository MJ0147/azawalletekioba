## 1. Background

Edo verbs divide broadly into **action verbs** and **adjectival verbs**. The division is not
clear-cut — syntactically they behave alike in many respects — but some adjectival verbs
undergo relativization and tonal change that turn them into attributive adjectives, and
action verbs cannot do this.

Semantically, adjectival verbs (often erroneously called adjectives) give the **inherent
quality or attribute** of the persons and objects they co-occur with.

| # | Edo | Gloss | Literal |
|---|-----|-------|---------|
| (1) | Òsàrọ́ khìán | 'Osaro is going' | Osaro go |
| (2) | Òsàrọ́ zùrọ́ | 'Osaro is foolish/stupid' | Osaro foolish/stupid |

In (1) the verb carries only the feature `[+VB]`. In (2) `zùrọ́` carries `[+VB]` **and**
`[+ADJ]` because of its attributive function — and it is this second function that makes
analysts mistake it for a pure adjective.

To function purely attributively it must undergo a transformation. Two routes:

**Reduplication**

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (3) | òkpìá ọ́kpá **zùrọ̀zùrọ̀** tótà yè ágá | 'one foolish/lazy man sat on the chair' |

**Relativization** — `nè ọ̀ zùrọ́` ('that he foolish') reduces to `nézúrọ̀`:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (4) | òkpìá **nézúrọ̀** lẹ̀ẹ́ fùá | 'a foolish/lazy man ran away' |
| (5) | òkpìá **nézùrọ̀zùrọ̀** lẹ̀ẹ́ fùá | 'a foolish/lazy man ran away' |

The author notes a more plausible underlying form for `nézúrọ̀` — one that also accounts for
the tone changes on *u* and *ọ* — is the relativizer `nè` plus the derived nominal
`èzúrọ̀` 'foolish person'. Either way, relativization underlies it.

Attributive adjectives **cannot** be derived from action verbs this way:

> (6) \*Òsàrọ́ nèkhìán… — *'Osaro that is going…'* — **ungrammatical**

### Why "adjectival verbs" and not "stative verbs"

The author prefers "adjectival verbs" to Chafe's [1970:88–89] **stative verbs**, because some
fail the "rule of thumb" test of answering *"What happened?"* / *"What is happening?"* —
`Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́rè` and `Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́` translate literally as "Osaro happied" and "Osaro is
happying".

There is a **cline** from inherently action verbs to inherently adjectival ones, with verbs
that are partly both in between.

## 2. Characteristics of the Edo verb system

### 2.1 Occurrence in the predicate

Edo is basically **SVO**. An action verb or an adjectival verb occurs in the frame
`# NP – (NP) #`, where `#` is a sentence boundary.

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (7) | òkhùò nìí mòsé | 'that woman is beautiful' |
| (8) | Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́ èghìè vbè ègùàè | 'Osaro was happy for his title in the palace' |

(8) also shows a prepositional phrase occurring after the object NP.

A **predicative adjective** derives from (7) by reduplication plus a copula:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (9) | òkhùò nìí **yè** mòsèmòsè | 'that woman is beautiful' |

A reduced relative clause in which the antecedent noun's pronoun does not occur gives a
**modifying adjective**:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (10) | òkhùò **némòsèmòsè** tótà | 'a beautiful woman is sitting' |

**Intensity.** Reduplication carries an intensity implication. The final vowel of `mòsé` can
also be lengthened, for both adjective formation and intensity:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (11) | òkhùò nìí yè **mòsèè** | 'that woman is intensively beautiful' |

`ghọ̀ghọ́` 'happy' in (8) **cannot** undergo these transformations, because it is partly action
and partly adjectival — it describes both the state of being happy and what Osaro did.
Pure action verbs such as `gó` 'shout', `lé` 'cook' and `mìẹ̀kùé` 'admit' cannot either.

### 2.2 Consonant commencing

Like all Edo verbs, adjectival verbs **begin with consonants and end with vowels**. The common
syllable structures are CV (monosyllabic), CVV and CVCV (disyllabic).

**(12) Adjectival verbs by syllable structure**

|----|-------|-----|-------|------|-------|
| kọ́n | foolish | màá | good | kpọ̀lọ́ | big |
| dìn | brave | khùá | heavy | khèrhé | small |
| tọ́n | hot | bàá | red | dìmwí | deep |
| tán | long/tall | sìé | black | hòghá | light |
| yó | high | vbòó | ripe | pẹ̀rhẹ́ | low |

| vbẹ́ | wide | tùá | tight | wẹ̀nrẹ́n | slim |
| bún | many/much | khọ̀ọ́ | hostile | mòsé | beautiful |

suffix other verbs take. **The vowel of the verb stem largely determines the vowel of the
suffix.**

| sìé | black | sìé+rè |
| fú | calm/quiet | fú+rù |

### Phonology behind the rule

**(15) Vowel inventory**

- Oral: `i  u  e  o  ẹ  ọ  a`
- Nasalized: `in  un  ẹn  ọn  an`

**(16) Distinctive features**

| | i | e | ẹ | a | ọ | o | u | in | ẹn | an | ọn | un |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | + | + | − | − | − | + | + | + | − | − | − | + |
| back | − | − | − | − | + | + | + | − | − | − | + | + |
| low | − | − | + | + | + | − | − | − | + | + | + | − |
| round | − | − | − | − | + | + | + | − | − | − | + | + |
| nasal | − | − | − | − | − | − | − | + | + | + | + | + |

Given how frequently **-re** occurs, the author treats it as the **underlying form**
(see also Amayo [1976]). All **low** vowels take `-re`. Low **nasalized** vowels first undergo
a nasal-lowering rule, because **/en/ does not occur in Edo**. **High** vowels assimilate the
features of /e/.

**(17) Rules**

- **a. Quality and Nasality Assimilation**
  `e → [αhigh, <βround>, <βback>, γnasal] / [αhigh, <−low>, βback, γnasal] r __ [Past suffix]`
- **b. Nasal Vowel Lowering**
  `V[−high, +nasal] → [+low]`

### Where the past suffix surfaces

The simple past suffix is **only manifest when the verb occurs sentence-finally**. There is no
obvious phonological reason for the deletion, so the author takes it as a **grammatical rule**
that `-re` and its variants are deleted before object noun phrases. Transitive verbs do not
take `-re` except when used intransitively.

| # | Edo | Gloss | |
|---|-----|-------|---|
| (18) | Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́rè | 'Osaro was happy' | grammatical |
| (19) | \*Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́rè èghìè | 'Osaro was happy for a title' | **ungrammatical** |

Time adverbials, prepositional phrases and embedded sentences tend to violate the rule by
occurring *after* the suffix:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (20) | àlìmoí vbóró **nódẹ̀** | 'an orange became ripe yesterday' |
| (21) | àlìmoí vbóró **vbè òkhùàè** | 'an orange became ripe in the basket' |
| (22) | àlìmoí ná vbóró **àdéghẹ̀ ù hòó** | 'this orange is ripe if you want' |

### 2.4 Comparative constructions

Comparison uses **verb serialization**, with `sẹ̀ẹ́` 'pass' / 'more than' post-modifying the
preceding verb. `sẹ̀ẹ́` can also stand alone as the main verb and still be comparative.

| # | Edo | Gloss | Verb type |
|---|-----|-------|-----------|
| (23) | Òsàsẹ́rè gbé **sẹ̀ẹ́** Òsàgié kèvbè Ìvìè | 'Osasere dances better than Osagie and Ivie' | action |
| (24) | Òsàsẹ́rè tán **sẹ̀ẹ́** Òsàgié kèvbè Ìvìè | 'Osasere is taller than Osagie and Ivie' | adjectival |

Omoregie [1983:57] identifies `sẹ́hìá` 'more than all' / 'pass all' for superlatives. **The
author disagrees:** there are no number restrictions on `sẹ̀ẹ́` in Edo, so positing `sẹ́hìá` for
the superlative is unnecessary.

### 2.5 Occurrence with auxiliaries

Adjectival verbs can be preceded by auxiliaries, which modify them and mark tense exactly as
they do for action verbs.

| # | Edo | Gloss | Literal |
|---|-----|-------|---------|
| (25) | Íràn **ghá** ghọ̀ghọ́ | 'they will be happy' | they Aux-Fut happy |
| (26) | Òsàrọ́ **rá** vbè ùvún nìí | 'Osaro is going to widen that hole' | Osaro Aux+Fut widen hole that |

`ghá` and `rá` are variants of the Edo future auxiliary. `rá` commonly indicates that the
action is **about to commence**. Amayo [1980] distinguishes the two senses of `rá` by positing
a **downstepped tone** (written `!`) before the verb:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (27a) | ọ̀ rá **!**kpòló | 'he will sweep' — *more distant future* |
| (27b) | ọ̀ rá kpòló | 'he is about to sweep' |

### 2.6 Nominalization

All Edo **nouns** begin *and* end with vowels; all **verbs** begin with consonants and end with
vowels. So nominalizing a verb obligatorily **prefixes a vowel**.

**(28) Action verbs → nominals**

| Stem | Gloss | Nominal | Gloss |
|------|-------|---------|-------|
| gìẹ́ | laugh | ògìẹ́ | act of laughing |
| dá | drink | èdá | act of drinking |
| tótà | sit down | ìtótà | act of sitting down |
| tuẹ́ | greet | òtuẹ́ | act of greeting |
| vẹ́n | wrestle | èvẹ́n | act of wrestling |

## 3. Adjectival relative clauses

> The opening of this section, including example (32), is **missing from the scans**.

**(33) Derivation of a relative clause** — `ìkhùò` 'women', `lẹ́rè` 'run+Past',
`ènà khìn` 'these-ones be':

| Stage | S₁ | S₂ | |
|-------|----|----|---|
| a. Base | [ìkhùò] | [ìkhùò lẹ́rè] | ènà khìn |
| b. Pronominalization | [ìkhùò] | [íràn lẹ́rè] | *they run+Past* |
| c. Relativizer insertion | [ìkhùò] | [nè íràn lẹ́rè] | *that they run+Past* |
| d. Elision | [ìkhùò] | [ní lẹ́rè] | *that-they run+Past* |

Relativization applies because of the **co-referential relation** between the NP in the matrix
sentence and the NP of the embedded sentence.

Adjectival relative clauses — which Omoregie [1983:49–58] **erroneously calls adjectives** —
follow the same derivation:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (34) | Òsàrọ́ rhié òkhùò **nọ̀mósé** | 'Osaro married a beautiful woman' |

| Stage | S₁ | S₂ |
|-------|----|----|
| a. Base | [Òsàrọ́ rhié òkhùò] | [òkhùò mòsé] |
| b. Pronominalization | [Òsàrọ́ rhié òkhùò] | [ọ̀ mòsé] *she beautiful* |
| c. Relativizer insertion | [Òsàrọ́ rhié òkhùò] | [nè ọ̀ mòsé] *that she beautiful* |
| d. Elision | [Òsàrọ́ rhié òkhùò] | [nọ̀mòsé] |

### Number agreement

The **singular or plural form of the antecedent NP** in the matrix sentence determines the
number of the adjectival relative clause. Had `ìkhùò` 'women' appeared in S₁ of (34),
relativization of S₂ would have produced `nìmòsé`.

What changes `ọ̀` in `nọ̀mòsé` to `ì` in `nìmòsé` is the pluralization and pronominalization of
the NP in the relative clause, which is in identity with the antecedent NP. The underlying form
of `nìmòsé` is `nè íràn mòsé`, where `íràn` 'they' is the plural counterpart of `ọ̀` 'he/she/it'.
Elision deletes `è` from `nè` and `-ràn` from `íràn`.

**(35) Singular / plural adjectival relative clauses**

| Singular | Plural | Gloss |
|----------|--------|-------|
| nọ́yó | níyó | high/tall |
| nọ́huánrẹ̀n | níhuánrẹ̀n | holy |
| nọ́fuá | nífuá | white |
| nọ́wánrẹ̀n | níwánrẹ̀n | elder |
| nọ̀bàá | nìbàá | red |
| nọ̀khọ̀rhiọ́n | nìkhọ̀rhiọ́n | ugly |
| nọ́khuá | níkhuá | grand/big |
| nọ́bígọ́ | níbílógọ́ | bent |
| nọ́tánrẹ̀n | nítánrẹ̀n | tall/long |
| nọ́khiọ́maẹ̀n | níkhiọ́maẹ̀n | old |

**Two notes on the morphology above**

- The past suffix **-rẹ̀n** obligatorily occurs in `nọ́huánrẹ̀n` 'holy', `nọ́wánrẹ̀n` 'elder' and
  `nọ́tánrẹ̀n` 'tall/long' as part of the derivation. It does **not** mean the state is past:
  `èmwá níhuánrẹ̀n tótà` means *'holy people are sitting'*, not that they were formerly holy.
  Compare the participial 'delighted' in English *'I am delighted to see you'*.
- In `níbílógọ́` 'bent', the **-ló** marks the plural/iterative of the stem `bí` 'push' →
  `bìló` 'push repeatedly'; `gó` 'curve', which follows it, gives the direction.

### Ordinal numbers — an irregular case

Some adjectival relative clauses do not follow the derivation above. `nọ́kàrò` 'first' and its
plural `níkàrò` derive regularly from `nè ọ́ kárò` and `nè íràn kárò`.

But `nọ́giévà` **cannot** derive from `*nè ọ̀ giévà`, because `*giévà` is not a lexical item in
Edo. Only `ógiévà` 'second' occurs, as an alternative way of saying `nọ́giévà`. The probable
source is `nè ọ̀ rè ógiévà` — literally *'that it be second'* — with elision deleting `e` from
`nè` and `ọ̀ rè` 'it be'. The plural would come from `nè íràn rè ógiévà` 'that they be second',
but elision reduces it to `nógiévà`, **identical to the singular**.

The author believes this derivation accounts for all cardinal numbers.

## 4. Adjectival qualifiers

**Real adjectives** functioning as noun qualifiers can be derived from some adjectival verbs by
**tonal change**, and in some cases **reduplication**. They can take the relative clause marker
`ne`, but the pronoun of the antecedent NP does not occur in them — **so they cannot be
pluralized**, unlike the adjectival relative clauses in §3.

**(36) Derivation table**

| Adjectival verb | Derived adjective | Adjectival relative clause | Derived adjective | Gloss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| khùìkhùì | — | nọ̀khùìkhùì | nékhùì | black |
| pẹ̀rhẹ́ | pẹ̀rhẹ̀ | nọ̀pẹ̀rhẹ́ | népẹ̀rhẹ̀ | low |
| wẹ̀nrẹ́n | wẹ̀nrẹ̀n | nọ̀wẹ̀nrẹ́n | néwẹ̀nrẹ̀n | slim |
| khèrhé | khèrhè | nọ̀khèrhé | nékhèrhè | small |
| khuẹ̀rhẹ́ | khuẹ̀rhẹ̀ | nọ̀khuẹ̀rhẹ́ | nékhuẹ̀rhẹ̀ | soft |
| mòsé | mòsèmòsè | nọ̀mòsé | némòsèmòsè | beautiful |
| muẹ̀muẹ́ | muẹ̀muẹ̀ | nọ̀muẹ̀muẹ́ | némuẹ̀muẹ̀ | weak/idle |
| pìrhí | pìrhìpìrhì | nọ̀pìrhí | népìrhìpìrhì | clumsy |
| sìkán | sìkànsìkàn | nọ̀sìkán | nésìkànsìkàn | stringy |
| tùnẹ́ | túnẹ́ | nọ̀tùnẹ́ | nétúnẹ́ | small |
| sùkú | sùkùsùkù | nọ̀sùkú | nésùkùsùkù | confused |
| hòghá | hóghá | nọ̀hòghá | néhóghá | light |
| zìghí | zìghìzìghì | nọ́zìghírì | nézìghìzìghì | disorderly |
| tìnniẹ́ | tínniẹ́ | nọ̀tìnniẹ́ | nétínniẹ́ | tiny |
| lùghú | lùghùlùghù | nọ́lùghúrù | nélùghùlùghù | disturb, e.g. dirty water |

Notes on this table:

- All the adjectival verbs above have the verb characteristics of §2.
- In nominalization, `khùìkhùì` becomes `ẹ́khùì` 'blackness' even though `*khùì` does not occur
  as a lexical item.
- Informants agree `sùkú` and `lùghú` **can** possibly be nominalized, but this is not
  frequently done — their nominals have probably fallen into disuse. All other adjectival verbs
  nominalize by vowel prefixing.
- The adjectival relative clauses pluralize: `nìkhùìkhùì`, `nìpẹ̀rhẹ́`, `nìwẹ̀nrẹ́n`, and so on.
  The adjectival verbs inside them retain their verbal characteristics — `nọ́zìghírì` and
  `nọ́lùghúrù` **obligatorily take the simple past suffix** in their derivation.

### Behaviour of derived adjectives

Derived adjectives **cannot occur alone** in a sentence except in the environment of the noun
they qualify, and they are **postposed** to that noun, as is common in Niger-Congo
[Welmers 1973:262]. They do **not** have the verb characteristics of §2 — the only exception
being that they begin with consonants and end with vowels.

Adjectives derived from adjectival verbs and those derived from adjectival relative clauses are
**interchangeable**, with no difference in semantic interpretation:

**(37)**

| From adjectival verb | From relative clause | Gloss |
|---|---|---|
| èvbàré khèrhè | èvbàré nékhèrhè | small food |
| ìrrí wẹ̀nrẹ̀n | ìrrí néwẹ̀nrẹ̀n | tiny rope |
| ódó pẹ̀rhẹ̀ | ódó népẹ̀rhẹ̀ | low mortar |
| òkhùò mòsèmòsè | òkhùò némòsèmòsè | beautiful woman |
| èmwá muẹ̀muẹ̀ | èmwá némuẹ̀muẹ̀ | weak people |

### Underived adjectives

A few Edo adjectives are **not** derived from adjectival verbs, and consequently cannot occur
in adjectival relative clauses. They are the second element in:

**(38)**

| Edo | Gloss | Adjective |
|-----|-------|-----------|
| ùkpó ọ́gbọ̀n | new year | ọ́gbọ̀n 'new' |
| èvbàré èsì | good food | èsì 'good' |
| ọ̀mwá dán | bad person | dán 'bad' |
| èmwá kpàtàkì | important people | kpàtàkì 'important' |
| ímọ́tò wọ̀rọ̀ | long car | wọ̀rọ̀ 'long' |
| èrhán túkpúrú | short tree | túkpúrú 'short' |

None of these can occur alone in a sentence without the noun they qualify preceding them.

### Welmers' warning — the diagnostic test

Whether a word is an adjective is decided by **whether it can stand alone**. `ọ́gbọ̀n` 'new'
**is** an adjective because standing alone is ungrammatical; its antonym `ọ̀wièyí` 'old' is
**not**, because it can stand alone.

| # | Edo | Gloss | |
|---|-----|-------|---|
| (39) | \*ọ́gbọ̀n èré Òsàrọ́ dérè | 'it is a new one Osaro bought' | **ungrammatical** |
| (40) | ọ̀wièyí èré Òsàrọ́ dérè | 'it is an old one Osaro bought' | grammatical |

`ọ̀wièyí` in (40) is a **nominal**, but it can also be used as an adjective:

| # | Edo | Gloss |
|---|-----|-------|
| (41) | ímọ́tò ọ̀wièyí èré Òsàrọ́ dérè | 'it is an old car Osaro bought' |

### 4.1 Ideophonic adjectival qualifiers

Ideophonic adjectival qualifiers are **particularly productive in Edo** for expressing
adjectival concepts. They suit free expression of a speaker's **idiosyncratic feelings** about
persons and objects — one speaker may describe a lazy, clumsy movement as `gùkọ̀gùkọ̀` where
another says `zùrọ̀zùrọ̀`. **Innovations and borrowings from other languages are common.**

They are formed by **reduplication**, and sometimes **obligatory triplication**.

Some forms already listed in (36) — `pìrhìpìrhì` 'clumsy', `sìkàn-sìkàn` 'stringy',
`zìghìzìghì` 'disorderly', `lùghùlùghù` 'disturb' — are in fact ideophonic qualifiers, but were
listed there because they derive from adjectival verbs. Those below **do not commonly have
verbal forms**:

**(42)**

| Ideophone | Gloss | Form |
|-----------|-------|------|
| sìlòsìlò | entangled | reduplicated |
| lìkpàlìkpà | rough surface | reduplicated |
| wẹ̀lẹ̀wẹ̀lẹ̀ | easy | reduplicated |
| bùyẹ̀bùyẹ̀ | weak | reduplicated |
| gúlúgúlú | deep | reduplicated |
| yàmàyàmà | ill-mannered | reduplicated |
| gbàkàgbàkà | strong/healthy | reduplicated |
| dúdúdú | quite dark | **triplicated (obligatory)** |
| sẹ̀sẹ̀sẹ̀ | spotless/immaculate white | **triplicated (obligatory)** |
| kánkánkán | strong/hard | **triplicated (obligatory)** |

The last three are **obligatorily triplicated** — `*dudu`, `*sẹsẹ` and `*kankan` are not
possible.

In a sentence:

| # | Edo | Gloss | Literal |
|---|-----|-------|---------|
| (43) | òkpìá yàmàyàmà èré Òzó khìn | 'Ozo is an ill-mannered man' | man ill-mannered it-is Ozo be |

Like other Edo adjectives, ideophonic qualifiers **follow the nouns they qualify** and **cannot
occur alone**.

**Reduplication for number agreement.** Some ideophonic qualifiers reduplicate to agree in
number with the noun they qualify:

| # | Edo | Gloss | Number |
|---|-----|-------|--------|
| (44) | Òsàrọ́ bièlé èmọ́ **gbàkàgbàkà** | 'Osaro gave birth to strong/healthy children' | plural |
| (45) | Òsàrọ́ bìẹ́ ọ̀mọ́ **gbàkà** | 'Osaro gave birth to a strong/healthy child' | singular |

Reduplication occurs in (44) because of the plural noun `èmọ́` 'children'. The verb `bìẹ́`
'give birth' also takes the suffix **-le** for the same purpose.

## Teaching notes for the Academy

Four distinct ways Edo expresses an adjectival concept, worth teaching as separate patterns:

1. **Adjectival verb in the predicate** — `òkhùò nìí mòsé` 'that woman is beautiful'.
   No copula. This is the base form.
2. **Reduplication + copula `yè`** → predicative — `òkhùò nìí yè mòsèmòsè`.
   Lengthening the final vowel (`mòsèè`) adds intensity.
3. **Relative clause with `nè`**, reduced by elision → `nọ̀mòsé` / `némòsèmòsè`.
   **This is the only one that inflects for number** (`nọ̀-` singular, `nì-`/`ní-` plural).
4. **Ideophonic qualifier** — expressive, open-class, reduplicated or triplicated.

Rules learners must not get wrong:

- Adjectives and qualifiers are **postposed** — the noun always comes first.
- No Edo adjective in this paper can **stand alone** without its noun. (`ọ̀wièyí` 'old' can,
  which is exactly why the author argues it is a nominal, not an adjective.)
- The past suffix **disappears before an object NP** — `Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́rè` but
  `*Òsàrọ́ ghọ̀ghọ́rè èghìè`.
- `-rẹ̀n` inside `nọ́huánrẹ̀n` 'holy' and `nọ́tánrẹ̀n` 'tall' is **derivational, not past tense**.
  Do not translate it as past.
- Comparison is by **serialization with `sẹ̀ẹ́`**, for both action and adjectival verbs.
  The author rejects a separate superlative `sẹ́hìá`.

(28–31), singular ↔ plural relative clauses (35), adjectival verb → derived adjective (36),
and noun + adjective pairs (37, 38).
