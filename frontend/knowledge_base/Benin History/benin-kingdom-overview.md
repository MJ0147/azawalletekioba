## The Benin Kingdom

Questions this answers: Benin Kingdom, Edo Kingdom, Benin history, Bini, Edo people, Edo State.

The Benin Kingdom (Edo Kingdom) is one of Africa's oldest monarchies, in present-day Edo State,
Nigeria. The earlier replies date its founding to about 900 AD. The line of Obas in the museum
catalogue begins with Oba Eweka I around 1200. The kingdom reached a golden age under Oba Ewuare
the Great (about 1440–1473). It is famous for its bronze castings, ivory carvings and oral
traditions, and the Oba's kingship continues today.

## Queen Idia

Questions this answers: Queen Idia, Queen Mother, Iyoba, Idia mask, Idia pendant.

Queen Idia was the first Iyoba (Queen Mother) of Benin and the mother of Oba Esigie (around 1504).
Oba Esigie created the Iyoba title. She led warriors to defend the kingdom and is celebrated as a
symbol of female power and royal courage. The Idia ivory mask is a masterpiece of Benin art, and one
is held in the British Museum. It inspired EKIOBA's IDIA Coin and the store's Idia Ivory Mask
Pendant.

## Benin bronze casting

Questions this answers: Benin Bronzes, bronze heads, brass casting, lost wax, Igun Eronmwon.

Benin bronze casting dates to the 13th century. It was mastered by the hereditary Igun Eronmwon
guild using the lost-wax technique. Casters made commemorative heads of Obas and Iyobas, court
plaques and ritual objects. Many bronzes were taken in the 1897 British punitive expedition, and
their return to Benin is an ongoing global conversation. EKIOBA's bronze replicas honour this living
tradition.

## Igue festival and Ugie Erha Oba

Questions this answers: Igue festival, Ugie, Ugie Erha Oba, royal festivals, ceremonies.

The Igue festival is the most sacred annual ceremony of the Benin Kingdom, held each December. The
Oba performs rites of royal renewal and honours the royal ancestors. The Ugie Erha Oba ceremony
honours the spirit of the Oba's father. Both feature royal regalia, court dances, music and
offerings.

## Coral beads (ivie)

Questions this answers: coral beads, ivie, royal coral, coral necklace, regalia.

Coral beads (*ivie* in Edo) are sacred symbols of royalty and spiritual protection. Only the Oba,
chiefs and senior title holders may wear particular coral arrangements, and wearing them without
rank is a cultural offence. Red coral was historically imported from the Mediterranean. The store's
Royal Coral Bead Necklace is made in the Benin style.
