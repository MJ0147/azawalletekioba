# Edo Question Formation: Polar, Alternative and Non-Polar Questions

Reference extracted from OCR text of a linguistics paper on how questions are formed in Edo
(Bini). Source material for the EKIOBA Edo Language Academy.

## 2.2 The question marker *yi*

**(4)**

| | As printed | Likely reading | Gloss | Translation |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| a. | Ösaro ghä rre. | Osaro gha rre. | Osaro AUX come | 'Osaro will come.' |
| b. | Osaro ghd rre yi? | Osaro gha rre yi? | Osaro AUX come Q | 'Will Osaro come?' |

*yi* does not work as a question marker in a negative sentence. Edo verbs are negated by one
particle, and nouns and pronouns by an enclitic particle. The OCR garbled both particles, but
the examples show the verb negator is **ma** (5, 10c, 10d) and the noun negator is **i**
(inside *Eire* 'it-NEG-be', 7). In a negative sentence, *yi* at the end of the sentence
"expresses the adverbial meaning of *before*":

**(5)** Ü ma rhie okhüö yi
'[You] have never married (a woman) before'. The first word of the translation is cut off.
*Ü* is probably 'you'. No gloss line is printed.

Besides marking questions, *yi* is used in **focus constructions**. Together with the focus
particle ***ere*** 'it-be', it gives extra prominence to the noun in focus:

**(6)**

| As printed | Likely reading | Gloss | Translation |
| - | - | - | - |
| Evbareere Ösarore yi? | Evbare ere Osaro re yi? | food it-be Osaro eat Q-EMPH | 'Is it food that Osaro is eating?' |

In a **negative** focus construction, *yi* is only an emphatic particle:

**(7)**

| As printed | Likely reading | Gloss | Translation |
| - | - | - | - |
| Eire evbare Ösaro re yi | Eire evbare Osaro re yi | it-NEG-be food Osaro eat EMPH | 'It is not food that Osaro is eating either'. |

**Conclusion:** at the end of a sentence, *yi* can stand for different words that happen to
sound the same. It is:
- a question marker in (4b)
- a time adverb ('before') in (5)
- a question marker and emphasis at once in (6)
- emphasis only in (7)

The question marker *yi* can also be added optionally to any non-polar question (§3).

## 2.3 Alternative questions with *ra*

This kind of Edo polar question is built from two or more sentences joined into one. They are
joined by ***ra*** 'or', which then becomes the only question marker in the sentence. Whenever
two sentences are joined by *ra*, the result must be read as a question.

The speaker makes a statement, then uses *ra* to join it to a second statement. The second
statement either negates the first or says something different.

**(8)**

| As printed | Likely reading | Gloss |
| - | - | - |
| Ösaro bo owä ra Ösärorhie bkhub? | Osaro bo owa ra Osaro rhie okhuo? | Osaro build house or-Q Osaro take woman |

'Did Osaro build a house or Osaro married a woman?' (translation as printed)

*ra* is glossed 'or-Q' because in (8) it both joins the sentences and marks the question.
*Ösaro* in the second sentence refers to the same person as in the first. So it can be
replaced by *o* 'he' without changing grammaticality or meaning.

*ra* can also join two or more **nouns**. Sentences with nouns joined this way are not
normally questions, unless they are said with raised pitch (§2.1):

**(9)** Osaro ra Osagie gha rre.
Osaro or Osagie AUX come — 'Osaro or Osagie will come.'

(8) contains two different statements, so can it really be called a polar question? The
author found that people answering it usually start with ***een*** 'yes' or ***eo*** 'no', then
give an answer. So all of these answers to (8) are well-formed:²

**(10)**

| | As printed | Likely reading | Translation |
| - | - | - | - |
| a. | Een, o bo owä | Een, o bo owa. | 'Yes, he built a house.' |
| b. | Een, o rhie okhüo. | Een, o rhie okhuo. | 'Yes, he married a woman.' (printed "8, he…") |
| c. | Eo, o mäbo owä. | Eo, o ma bo owa. | 'No, he did not build a house.' |
| d. | Eo, o md rhie bkhub. | Eo, o ma rhie okhuo. | 'No, he did not marry a woman.' |

### *ra* at the end of the sentence

The role of *ra* as a question marker, and why questions formed with it count as polar, is
clearer when the second sentence is deleted. Speakers often do this when they don't want to
suggest answers or limit how the listener can respond. Such alternative questions end in *ra*,
as in the following example. Strictly speaking, *ra* is no longer a conjunction here, so it is
glossed Q (question marker):

**(11)** Osaro bo owä ra? (printed with a stray "ou" after it)
Osaro build house Q — 'Did Osaro build a house?'

Welmers (1973:305–6) describes something similar in Fante: "there is a simple conjunction
/andd/ [as printed]; this, however, may also be used as a question marker at the end of a
yes-no question". He does not give the underlying forms of such questions.

The second sentence is not always deleted completely as in (11). It can also be shortened:

**(12)**

| As printed | Gloss as printed | Translation |
| - | - | - |
| Ovbokhän nie qnä khin ra otuen? | wife your this-one be or-Q relation-your | 'Is this your wife or your relation?' |

The gloss runs over two scrambled lines in the scan, so which gloss goes with which Edo word is
uncertain. *qnä* is probably *ọna* (the OCR often reads ọ as q).

An answer to (12) can also begin with *een* 'yes' or *eo* 'no', as in (10). In English, that
kind of answer might not fit.

So far, *ra* joins sentences, and the joined sentence must be read as a polar question. It can
also join nouns, and then the sentence is not a question (see (9)). The two can appear in the
same sentence. In this example the second sentence is deleted:

**(13)**

| As printed | Likely reading | Gloss | Translation |
| - | - | - | - |
| Öorä Ösagie gha rre ra? | Osaro ra Osagie gha rre ra? | Osaro or Osagie AUX come Q | 'Will Osaro or Osagie come?' |

From (13), *ra* 'or' and *ra* 'Q' are separate words underneath that are pronounced the same.

The question marker *ra* **cannot occur in embedded questions**. Adding it to the end of (14)
makes the sentence ungrammatical:

**(14)** O no omwe adeghe u ghä ne.
he ask me whether you AUX come — 'He asked me whether you will come.'
(The source glosses *ne* as 'come' here, and *rre* as 'come' in (4), (9) and (13). Both are
recorded as printed. The project owner confirms the Edo word for 'come' is **lare**. That is
what the academy vocabulary uses.)

## 3. Non-polar questions

The paper covers five ways to form non-polar questions in Edo. The same question can often be
asked in more than one way. In all five, the question word *yi* can optionally be added at the
end of the sentence as a second question marker.

### 3.1 The *de* + NP + S pattern

A common way to form a non-polar question is to put the question particle ***de*** before the
noun phrase being asked about. The information about that noun phrase comes next, in a
relative clause. *yi* can optionally be added at the end.

**(15)**

| | As printed | Likely reading | Gloss | Translation |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| a. | De ehe ne Ösäro tie (yi)? | De ebe ne Osaro tie (yi)? | what/which book REL Osaro read Q | 'What/which book is Osaro reading?' |
| b. | De Osaro ne o tie ehe (yi)? | De Osaro ne o tie ebe (yi)? | which Osaro REL PRO read book Q | 'Which Osaro is reading a book?' |

*ehe* is OCR for *ebe* 'book'. Example (16) prints it correctly.

To explain these questions, you need to consider what the person asking already assumes, and
the grammatical role of the part being asked about. In both questions, the asker assumes:

**(16)** Osaro tie ebe.
Osaro read book — 'Osaro is reading a book.'

The parts being asked about are *ebe* 'book' in (15a) and *Osaro* in (15b). *de* puts these
noun phrases in focus, and (16) is embedded like this:³

**(17)**

| | Likely reading | Gloss |
| - | - | - |
| a. | De ebe S[Osaro tie ebe] (yi) | what/which book [Osaro read book] |
| b. | De Osaro S[Osaro tie ebe] (yi) | which Osaro [Osaro read book] |

*The text ends here.*
